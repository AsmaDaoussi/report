\chapter*{General Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{General Introduction}

The global video game industry has experienced remarkable growth over the past decade, evolving from a niche entertainment segment into one of the most lucrative creative economies in the world. With revenues surpassing hundreds of billions of dollars annually and a continuously expanding ecosystem of developers, studios, and independent creators, the demand for high-quality digital assets has never been greater. Beyond the games themselves, a significant and often overlooked opportunity lies not in the creation of video games directly, but in building the infrastructure that supports them, namely, digital marketplaces dedicated to the exchange of 3D assets. These platforms serve as essential bridges between creators and consumers, enabling studios, developers, architects, and designers to access a vast library of ready-made models, textures, animations, and environments. The rise of real-time 3D technologies, game engines such as Unity and Unreal Engine, and the expanding metaverse economy has further accelerated the need for structured, accessible, and reliable 3D asset marketplace solutions. Investing in such platforms represents a strategic and high-value proposition in today's digital economy, as they reduce production costs, shorten development cycles, and democratize access to professional-grade visual content.

For this purpose, this project introduces a unified 3D digital marketplace — a centralized platform, or \textit{vitrine}, designed to connect creators and consumers of 3D assets within a single, cohesive ecosystem. The platform caters to both internal users, such as affiliated studios, developers, and in-house teams, and external users, including independent designers, clients, and businesses seeking ready-to-use digital content. Through this marketplace, users can seamlessly browse, upload, purchase, and sell 3D assets across a variety of categories, with a streamlined experience that prioritizes accessibility, quality, and trust. By unifying these interactions under one platform, the solution aims to address the fragmentation currently characterizing the 3D asset market, offering a reliable, scalable, and user-centered alternative to existing tools.

This report documents the different steps of the project towards its realization. It starts with \textbf{Chapter 1}, which establishes the general project context, the host organization, a comparative study of existing solutions, the chosen methodology, and the technology stack. \textbf{Chapter 2} details the requirements analysis, system actors, functional and non-functional requirements, and global system architecture. \textbf{Chapter 3} covers Sprint 0 — the research, planning, and architectural decisions made before any code was written. \textbf{Chapters 4 and 5} document Sprints 1 through 4, delivering the core platform: authentication, RBAC, asset catalog, payment, reviews, and administration. \textbf{Chapters 6 and 7} cover Sprints 5 through 8, delivering the IGS Workspace, AI integration, the recommendation engine, and the final UI overhaul. \textbf{Chapter 8} presents the CI/CD pipeline, Docker containerization, and production deployment strategy.
