\section*{Introduction}
This chapter presents the sprints that transform the catalog into a controlled marketplace.
Sprint 3 focuses on seller upload, asset moderation, reviews, ratings, notifications, and
subscriptions. Sprint 4 introduces role-specific business workflows: intern asset requests,
vouchers, peer-to-peer trades, seller analytics, and community groups.

\section{Sprint 3: Upload, Moderation and Trust}

\subsection{Sprint Backlog}

\begin{sprintbox}{Sprint 3 -- Seller and Moderation Workflow}
\textbf{Goal:} allow sellers to submit assets and allow internal reviewers to control catalog
quality before publication.
\end{sprintbox}

\subsection{Analysis and Design}

The upload workflow is intentionally moderated. A submitted asset is created with a
\texttt{pending} status and becomes visible in the public catalog only after approval.
Rejected assets keep a rejection reason so the seller understands the correction needed.
Approved assets cannot be edited freely; instead, sellers submit an edit request, which is
reviewed by an authorized internal user.

Reviews and ratings are linked to ownership. This prevents users from reviewing assets that
they have not purchased or unlocked. Ratings update aggregated counters on the asset record,
while reviews can also receive AI sentiment metadata.

\subsection{Implementation}

File upload and storage are handled by the upload route and the storage service. Supabase
Storage keeps binary asset files and thumbnails, while PostgreSQL stores metadata,
moderation status, tags, formats, and ownership information. Admin moderation is implemented
under \texttt{admin.js} and \texttt{adminController.js}; asset edit requests use
\texttt{assetEditRequestRepository.js}.

The review system uses \texttt{reviewController.js} and \texttt{reviewRepository.js}. The
notification system stores persistent notifications in PostgreSQL and exposes notification
routes used by the frontend \texttt{NotificationContext.jsx} and navigation panel.

\subsection{Sprint Review}

At the end of Sprint 3, the marketplace had a credible trust layer: assets could be submitted
and moderated, users could review owned assets, administrators had moderation tools, and
notifications kept users informed about workflow changes.

\section{Sprint 4: Intern Workflow and Community Modules}

\subsection{Sprint Backlog}

\begin{sprintbox}{Sprint 4 -- Role-Specific Advanced Features}
\textbf{Goal:} make the RBAC model operational through intern requests, vouchers, groups,
trades, and seller-oriented analytics.
\end{sprintbox}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Sprint 4 Backlog}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{C{1.1cm} X C{2cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{User Story} & \textbf{Priority} \\
\midrule
US-18 & As an intern, I can request access to a paid asset with a justification. & High \\
US-19 & As an admin or employee, I can approve or reject intern asset requests. & High \\
US-20 & As an admin, I can generate vouchers for controlled asset access. & Medium \\
US-21 & As a user, I can redeem a valid voucher. & Medium \\
US-22 & As a user, I can create or join groups. & Low \\
US-23 & As a user, I can propose an asset trade to another user. & Low \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\subsection{Analysis and Design}

The intern workflow was designed for a studio context where interns may need paid assets but
should not purchase them personally. The request stores the intern, target asset, message,
status, reviewer, and review note. After approval, a voucher can be created and redeemed to
unlock the asset in the user's library.

Groups and trades add a community dimension to the marketplace. Groups organize users around
shared interests, while trades allow users to propose exchanges between assets.

\subsection{Implementation}

Intern requests are implemented through \texttt{intern.js},
\texttt{internAssetRequestRepository.js}, and admin moderation endpoints. Voucher logic is
encapsulated in \texttt{voucherRepository.js}. Groups and trades are implemented through
\texttt{groups.js}, \texttt{trades.js}, \texttt{groupController.js}, and
\texttt{tradeController.js}.

Frontend pages such as \texttt{VoucherRedeem.jsx}, \texttt{Group.jsx}, \texttt{Trade.jsx},
and \texttt{Analytics.jsx} expose these workflows while respecting route guards and
permissions.

\subsection{Sprint Review}

Sprint 4 completed the advanced marketplace workflows. The platform became more than a
catalog: it supported internal asset access, controlled vouchers, community interaction, and
seller-oriented visibility.

\section*{Conclusion}
Sprints 3 and 4 introduced the governance and collaboration mechanisms required for a
professional asset marketplace. They strengthened trust, moderation, internal control, and
community engagement.
