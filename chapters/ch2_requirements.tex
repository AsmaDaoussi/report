\section*{Introduction}
This chapter defines the functional scope of the IGS Marketplace project. It identifies the
actors, describes functional and non-functional requirements, presents the product backlog
used to drive the Scrum iterations, and summarizes the main UML design views required
before implementation.

% ---- 2.1 ACTORS ----
\section{Actors Identification}

The platform is used by external marketplace users and by internal IGS collaborators. Access
rights are controlled through a role-based access control model (RBAC), where each role
receives a set of permissions adapted to its responsibilities.

% ---- 2.2 NEEDS IDENTIFICATION ----
\section{Needs Identification}

Following the presentation style used in the reference report, the needs are captured from
the point of view of the system actors. They are separated into functional needs, which
describe what the system must do, and non-functional needs, which describe quality and
technical constraints.

\subsection{Functional Requirements}

\textbf{Visitor needs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Browse the public catalog without authentication.
  \item Search and filter assets by category, price, rating, file type, and availability.
  \item Consult asset details, previews, descriptions, formats, tags, seller information, and reviews.
  \item Create an account using email/password or OAuth authentication.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Member needs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Authenticate securely and manage personal profile information.
  \item Add assets to the cart, remove them, and proceed to checkout.
  \item Purchase paid assets through a payment provider and claim free assets.
  \item Access purchased or unlocked assets from a personal library.
  \item Rate, review, like, and download assets when access rights allow it.
  \item Subscribe to a plan and consult subscription history.
  \item Join groups, propose trades, and receive in-app notifications.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Seller needs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Request seller access and wait for administrative validation.
  \item Upload assets with files, thumbnails, formats, metadata, price, and tags.
  \item Follow moderation status and correct rejected submissions.
  \item Request edits on already approved assets.
  \item Consult personal asset statistics and seller analytics.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Intern and internal collaborator needs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Access the IGS Workspace according to internal role permissions.
  \item Request access to paid marketplace assets with a justification.
  \item Redeem vouchers generated by authorized reviewers.
  \item Collaborate through internal projects, tasks, documents, dashboards, and real-time chat.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Administrator needs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Manage users, roles, permissions, role requests, and account status.
  \item Moderate submitted assets, edit requests, intern uploads, and intern asset requests.
  \item Manage vouchers, subscriptions, categories, commission configuration, and platform settings.
  \item Consult platform analytics, audit logs, orders, notifications, and moderation history.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{AI-assisted needs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Analyze review sentiment and help moderators identify suspicious or useful feedback.
  \item Generate asset recommendations from user events such as views, likes, downloads, and purchases.
  \item Provide chatbot assistance for marketplace questions and navigation.
\end{itemize}

architecture before the detailed sprint realization chapters. The project follows a

\subsection{Software Architecture}

The backend is organized around a layered route-controller-repository structure. Routes
expose REST endpoints and attach middleware. Controllers implement use cases and coordinate
services. Repositories encapsulate SQL queries and protect the rest of the application from
database details. Cross-cutting services such as storage, audit logging, recommendations,
AI inference, and token management are reused by several controllers.

On the frontend, the Marketplace and IGS Workspace are independent React applications.
They share the same backend API but keep their own routing, layout, page components, context
providers, and services. This separation keeps the public marketplace experience distinct
from the internal collaboration workspace.

\section{Design Patterns}

The codebase uses several recurring design patterns and architectural patterns:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Design Patterns Used in the Project}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{L{3.4cm} L{4.2cm} X}
\toprule
\textbf{Pattern} & \textbf{Project Location} & \textbf{Role in the System} \\
\midrule
Three-tier Architecture & React apps, Express API, PostgreSQL/Supabase & Separates presentation, business logic, and persistence concerns. \\
\midrule
Layered Architecture & \texttt{routes}, \texttt{controllers}, \texttt{repositories} & Keeps HTTP routing, business rules, and SQL access independent. \\
\midrule
Repository Pattern & \texttt{MarketPlace\_Back/src/repositories} & Centralizes database operations for users, assets, orders, reviews, groups, trades, vouchers, and workspace data. \\
\midrule
Middleware / Chain of Responsibility & \texttt{auth.js}, \texttt{permissions.js}, \texttt{validator.js}, \texttt{rateLimiters.js} & Applies authentication, authorization, validation, and rate limits before controllers execute. \\
\midrule
Service Layer & \texttt{storageService}, \texttt{auditService}, \texttt{aiService}, \texttt{recommendationService} & Encapsulates reusable business services and external integrations. \\
\midrule
Singleton Configuration & \texttt{database.js}, \texttt{supabase.js} & Shares one configured database pool and Supabase client across the backend. \\
\midrule
Provider Pattern & React context providers & Shares authentication, cart, permissions, notifications, filters, and user data across components. \\
\midrule
Route Guard & \texttt{ProtectedRoute.jsx}, \texttt{Can.jsx} & Protects frontend pages and UI actions according to authentication and permissions. \\
\midrule
Observer / Pub-Sub & Socket.io chat and notifications & Broadcasts chat messages and real-time events to subscribed clients. \\
\midrule
Adapter Pattern & Frontend service files and backend external service wrappers & Hides API, payment, storage, and AI-provider details behind stable local functions. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
